Kind Subscription

Problem: on Avito you could only help charities once at a time — to keep supporting them you had to go through the whole flow again

Solution: I designed monthly giving: from choosing a charity and subscribing to managing the subscription in the profile

Role
Owned the whole recurring giving flow: subscription, charity reports, payment and thank-you
Team
Product manager, project manager, backend, frontend, editor
Collaboration
Aligned payment methods with the payments team, and entry points with the charity team and the listing-creation team

About the project

Kind Subscription is a monthly donation to a charity in Avito’s charity section. A person picks a charity and an amount, and the following payments go through automatically.

Regular income helps charities plan their work. For the donor, it matters to know up front who gets the money, when the next payment happens and how to change or cancel the subscription.

In research, people asked for reports and concrete results of the charities’ work. So we added information about the charity before the subscription step.

Problems

The flow had to be built around three goals

01

Let people give regularly without going through the whole flow again

02

Show the charity’s work before payment so people can decide whom to trust with their help

03

Unify payment and thank-you screens: they looked different across charity flows

From one-off help to a subscription

Put the charity, the amount and a “One-off / Monthly” switch on one screen. Previously choosing the charity and the amount were split across two

Concepts

Tested a 3D character on the subscription screen and confetti on the thank-you screen. The character took up half the screen, so for setup I chose a restrained version where the terms are visible right away. The emotional animation stayed after payment

Entry points

The subscription shows up in listing search results, on the charity landing page and after posting a listing. In each place the offer respects what the person came to do

After posting a listing, we offer to help once the main task is done. In search results a compact banner introduces the subscription, and the charity landing page explains the terms in more detail.

Choosing a charity

Moved choosing the charity to the first screen: before picking an amount you can learn about the charity, switch to another one and open its “Good deeds” reports

For one-off donations we suggest charities from random categories, so that less popular causes get help too, not just children and animals.

Subscribing

The setup screen brings together the charity, the amount and the monthly charge. You can pick 100, 300 or 500 ₽ or enter your own amount; the note about the recurring payment is visible before subscribing

The details moved to “How it works”: no fees, money goes directly to charities, a monthly “Kindness badge”, changing and cancelling the subscription. The main screen stays compact.

Thank-you after payment

Unified the thank-you across charity flows: confirming the action, the person’s contribution and a thank-you. The approach works full screen, in a bottom sheet and in a modal

For the subscription, the screen confirms the monthly help and its terms. I added a hearts animation and two actions: share, and add a “Kindness badge” to your listing.

About 2,000 thank-you views a day — that’s across all charity flows, not just the subscription.

Managing the subscription

The profile shows the charity, the amount and the next payment date. You can change the amount right in the card and cancel the subscription in settings. It can also be managed from the “Kindness badge” page

In the MVP, switching charities requires cancelling and subscribing again — a limitation of the YooKassa payment system. Switching without cancelling is planned as the next step.

Results

Launched recurring giving with subscribing and subscription management. Choosing a charity now comes with information about its work

≈55

New subscriptions a day

+64%

Average payment above the minimum

≈1,300

Charity report views a day

These numbers describe product usage. To judge how sustainable recurring giving is, the next step is to measure repeat charges, cancellations and the conversion of each entry point.
